The Science

Cleaning exhaust by treating the air that goes in.

The Plasmoid X system fits between a diesel engine's air intake and its exhaust. It charges the incoming air, warms it with heat taken from the outgoing exhaust and feeds it to the turbo.

How It Works

The whole system, from the air intake to the exhaust outlet. Press Play to follow it through, or pick a stage.

  1. 1

    UV ionizer

    Fresh air is drawn in through a filter and along a tube lit with UV light, instead of going straight into the turbo.

  2. 2

    Bubbler

    The ionized air bubbles up through water and leaves as plasmoids. A filter keeps the water out of the engine.

  3. 3

    Vajra

    The plasmoid air spirals through the inner chamber of two linked stainless spheres. A sealed core sphere in each one keeps it swirling.

  4. 4

    Counter-flow heat exchange

    Hot exhaust spirals the opposite way through the outer chamber and passes heat through the wall into the plasmoids.

  5. 5

    Into the engine

    The warmed plasmoids feed the turbo inlet in place of ordinary intake air. They are designed to give a more complete burn.

  6. 6

    Exhaust out

    The exhaust, now cooler, leaves through the outer chamber and out of the boat.

    The UV ionizer, bubbler, pipe runs, turbo and engine are drawn to show how the system connects. They are not to scale.

    Better at higher loads. A harder-working engine runs hotter and moves more air, and both help the system produce plasmoids.

    A Different Approach

    SCR and particulate filters work on the exhaust. This works on the air.

    Conventional SCR and diesel particulate filter systems trap particulates and dose chemicals to neutralize NOx in the exhaust. They work, but they are bulky, heavy and need regular servicing.

    Plasmoid X treats the air before it goes into the engine, so it has no filter media of its own to clog. Where an engine is certified with emission equipment, changes to it need approval from the vessel's flag or class.

    The Aim

    What the system is designed to do

    A more complete burn

    The system works on the air before combustion, with the aim of burning more of the fuel inside the cylinder.

    Better fuel economy

    A more complete burn would mean less fuel used to do the same work.

    Longer engine life

    Less soot means less abrasive wear on cylinders and internals over time.
